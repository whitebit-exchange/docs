The transfer request takes no idempotency key, and two identical requests move the funds twice. A `200` response
means WhiteBIT received the request, and the transfer can still fail after the response. After a timeout or a `5xx`
response, the transfer can still complete. A record in the transfer history shows that the request was received, not
that the funds moved, so the history does not confirm a transfer.

The sub-account's own balance confirms a transfer: read `main` for the ticker on the sub-account. A transfer to the
sub-account raises it by the amount, and a transfer to the main account lowers it. The main account's balance
changes with transfers to and from every sub-account and does not confirm a single transfer. After a `200`, a timeout or a `5xx`,
read the sub-account's `main` until it shows the transfer, and do not resend while waiting. A transfer that does
not show is a manual check, not an automatic resend.

The caller cannot attach its own reference to a transfer, so an integration stores its own record of each transfer
before the call and compares the sub-account's `main` with the amount expected from its records of every movement on
the sub-account, including deposits and withdrawals. Send the next transfer for the same sub-account and ticker only
after the previous one shows.
