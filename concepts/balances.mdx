WhiteBIT uses three balance types to separate funds by purpose: Main, Trade, and Collateral. Funds must be transferred between balances before use — deposits arrive in Main, trading requires Trade or Collateral.

## Balance types

**Main balance** — The primary holding balance. Deposits arrive here. Withdrawals leave from here. Creating a U EARN (lending) investment debits funds from Main and moves the assets into a separate investment account; the platform UI surfaces the investment as a virtual Earn balance. Main balance is not used for direct trading.

- Endpoint: `POST /api/v4/main-account/balance` — [API Reference](/api-reference/account-wallet/overview)

**Trade balance (Spot)** — Holds funds for spot trading. Transfer funds from Main to Trade before placing spot orders. Each currency has an `available` amount (ready to trade) and a `freeze` amount (locked in open orders).

- Endpoint: `POST /api/v4/trade-account/balance` — [API Reference](/api-reference/account-wallet/overview)

**Collateral balance (Margin & Futures)** — Holds funds for both margin and futures trading. Transfer funds from Main to Collateral before opening leveraged positions. The same Collateral balance serves both products — the market pair determines whether the position is Margin or Futures.

- Endpoint: `POST /api/v4/collateral-account/balance` — [API Reference](/api-reference/trading/collateral-account-balance)

<Note>
  **API naming convention:** WhiteBIT's API uses "collateral" endpoints for both
  Margin and Futures trading. The market pair determines the product:
  spot pairs (e.g., `BTC_USDT`) for Margin, perpetual pairs (e.g., `BTC_PERP`)
  for Futures. All endpoints under `/api/v4/order/collateral/` and
  `/api/v4/collateral-account/` serve both products.
</Note>

## Fund flow

Transfers between balance types use a single endpoint and are fee-free. The endpoint returns `201` once the transfer is queued, and the funds show on the destination balance when the transfer completes.

```
Main ↔ Trade          (for spot trading)
Main ↔ Collateral     (for margin and futures trading)
Trade ↔ Collateral    (executed as two steps through Main)
```

Every transfer is stored with Main on one side. A direct Trade ↔ Collateral transfer is accepted — the platform executes such a transfer as two steps through Main, so transfer history returns two records, `spot → main` and `main → collateral`, each carrying the full amount. The platform UI exposes the same direct Trade ↔ Collateral transfer, and the same Main-routed steps happen under the hood.

- Transfer endpoint: `POST /api/v4/main-account/transfer` — [API Reference](/api-reference/account-wallet/transfer-between-balances)
- Transfer history endpoint: `POST /api/v4/main-account/transfer/history` — [API Reference](/api-reference/account-wallet/get-transfer-history)

Transfer direction is controlled via the `method` parameter:
- `deposit` — Main → Trade
- `withdraw` — Trade → Main
- `collateral-deposit` — Main → Collateral
- `collateral-withdraw` — Collateral → Main

## Available vs. frozen balance

Each balance entry includes two fields:

| Field | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| `available` | Funds ready to use for new orders or transfers |
| `freeze` | Funds locked by open orders or pending operations |

Total balance = `available` + `freeze`. Only `available` funds can be transferred or used to place orders.

## USDTB (bonus collateral)

USDTB is a bonus collateral token used for futures trading. USDTB cannot be deposited or withdrawn — the platform grants USDTB as a bonus asset. USDTB appears in the Collateral balance and counts toward futures margin requirements.

USDTB enters futures margin calculations through the futures-specific parameters:

- `futuresEquity` = `equity` + USDTB. Returned by [Collateral account summary](/api-reference/trading/collateral-account-summary).
- `futuresFreeMargin` = `freeMargin` + USDTB. Returned by [Collateral account summary](/api-reference/trading/collateral-account-summary).
- Futures Margin Fraction — includes the USDTB bonus balance. Used in the [ADL execution price](/concepts/auto-deleveraging#execution-price) formula.

## Balance normalization

Balance normalization is an automatic conversion inside the Collateral balance. When the balance of an asset in the Collateral balance goes negative, the platform places a system-generated order of [type `14`](/websocket/account-streams/overview#order-types) to settle the negative amount. A negative asset balance is the only trigger. Typical causes are realized funding, realized negative [PnL](/glossary#pnl), and fees on collateral orders.

Opening a position does not trigger normalization by itself, but the fee on the opening order can. Example: the Collateral balance holds only BTC and 0 USDT. An order on `BTC_USDT` charges the fee in USDT, the USDT balance goes negative, and a normalization order follows.

A borrow on its own does not trigger normalization. When the account closes a borrow, normalization runs as part of the closure.

Normalization always trades on a USDT-quoted market, `<ASSET>_USDT`. The side depends on which asset balance is negative:

| Negative balance | Normalization orders |
|------------------|----------------------|
| USDT | The platform sells a positive collateral asset for USDT on the `<ASSET>_USDT` market of that asset. |
| Another asset, for example BTC or ETH | The platform buys the missing asset with USDT on the market of that asset, such as `BTC_USDT` or `ETH_USDT`. |
| Another asset, with too little USDT to buy the asset | Two orders: the platform sells a positive asset for USDT on the `<ASSET>_USDT` market of that asset, then buys the missing asset with the proceeds. |

A normalization order always executes as the [taker](/glossary#taker), and the platform charges the account's taker fee. The order is not subject to [self-trade prevention](/platform/self-trade-prevention), so the order can match the account's own resting order. In that case, both sides of the trade belong to the account.

To avoid normalization, keep a buffer in the Collateral balance of each asset that fees, funding, and realized PnL settle in, such as USDT. Holding other assets, such as BTC, does not stop the USDT balance from going negative.

Normalization fills appear on two WebSocket account streams:

| Channel | How to identify a normalization fill |
|---------|--------------------------------------|
| [Orders Executed](/websocket/account-streams/orders-executed) | `type` `14`, `source` `"normalization"`, and an empty `client_order_id`. No `liquidation_` or `adl_` prefix. |
| [Deals](/websocket/account-streams/deals) | `deals_update` has no order-type field. Match the order ID in `params[3]` to the order `id` on `ordersExecuted_update`. |

## Deposit flow

1. Generate a deposit address. Use `POST /api/v4/main-account/create-new-address` ([API Reference](/api-reference/account-wallet/create-new-address-for-deposit)) to generate a unique address per request, or `POST /api/v4/main-account/address` ([API Reference](/api-reference/account-wallet/get-cryptocurrency-deposit-address)) to retrieve a persistent reusable address. Alternatively, create an address through the platform UI.
2. Send crypto to the generated address.
3. Funds arrive in Main balance once the network reaches the asset's `confirmations_for_trading` count.
4. Transfer from Main to Trade (for spot orders) or to Collateral (for margin/futures positions).

Until the network reaches the full `confirmations` count, the [Main balance](/api-reference/account-wallet/main-balance) endpoint reports the held amount as `pending_deposit_amount`. The account can move the held amount to the Trade balance and trade it. The platform blocks blockchain withdrawals of the held amount and transfers of the held amount to the Collateral balance. When the two counts are equal, the platform holds nothing. [Asset Status](/api-reference/market-data/asset-status-list) returns both counts per network.

## Withdrawal flow

1. Ensure funds are in Main balance — transfer from Trade or Collateral if needed.
2. (Optional) If the held currency differs from the withdrawal currency, transfer to Trade balance, convert via the [Convert](/platform/convert) feature, and transfer back to Main balance. See [Payment Integration](/guides/payment-integration) for the full withdrawal-with-conversion flow.
3. Submit a withdrawal request via `POST /api/v4/main-account/withdraw`.
4. Withdrawal processes after security checks complete.

<Note>
  **EEA users:** Crypto withdrawals require a `travelRule` object with beneficiary
  information (name, address, VASP). Deposits may be held for Travel Rule
  verification (status codes 27, 28). See [Regulatory Compliance](/institutional/compliance)
  for full details.
</Note>
